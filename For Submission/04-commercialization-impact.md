# Commercialization & Impact — One-Pager

**Project:** Not Enough Space · **Type:** Free, open educational PWA · **Audience:** ages 7–12,
parents, and teachers.

## The impact we are built for

The 2026 challenge asks us to introduce *school-age* enthusiasts to NASA's discarded
hardware. That audience is underserved: the authoritative data (NSSDCA) is engineer-facing,
and most child content about "space junk" is either cartoonish or absent. Not Enough Space
closes that gap with **data-accurate, play-based, accessible** storytelling.

- **Reach without friction.** A no-build, offline PWA installs to any tablet/laptop and
  works with no internet after first load — directly usable in under-resourced classrooms.
- **Inclusive by default.** Bilingual (EN/ES), keyboard/screen-reader ready, high-contrast,
  reduced-motion, TTS. Designed so *every* child can use it.
- **Measurable engagement loop.** Read → dive → collect → certificate gives teachers and
  parents a concrete artifact of learning.

## Why we are not charging

This is a NASA-public-data, education-focused product. A paywall would contradict the
mission and the audience. We are **free and open by design**. Sustainability therefore
comes from partnerships and institutional adoption, not consumer revenue.

## Sustainability / "commercialization" path

1. **Education partnerships.** Co-branded modules with **NASA Space Place** and science
   museums, where the "Space Junk Museum" becomes a digital exhibit companion.
2. **Educator toolkit (the realistic revenue-adjacent model).** A free app plus an
   optional *teacher pack* — printable worksheets, lesson plans aligned to science
   standards, and a classroom progress view. The pack can be funded by schools/districts
   or offered freemium.
3. **Open-source community.** MIT/Apache-licensed codebase invites teachers and developers
   to localize (the i18n layer already supports any language) and contribute chapters.
4. **Grant funding.** Eligible for STEM-education and digital-inclusion grants given the
   accessibility and low-bandwidth design.

## Risks & honesty

- We have **not yet run classroom pilots**; impact is currently qualitative. A lightweight
  educator feedback form and a small pilot are the immediate next steps to produce
  evidence.
- Adoption depends on discovery (search/app stores, museum referrals) — a distribution
  plan is part of the roadmap.

## One-line summary

> A free, offline, accessible game that turns NASA's abandoned hardware into a museum
> kids actually want to explore — sustainably scaled through education partnerships and
> an open-source community, not paywalls.
